Government Regulation (PP) No. 24 of 1997 concerning implementing provisions for government regulation no 24 of 1997 concerning Land Registration.


Chapter 2: Measurement and Mapping.
Part 3: Determination and Installation of Land Boundaries

Article section Description Notes
19
(1) For the purposes of determining land boundaries as referred to in Article 18 of Government Regulation Number 24 of 1997:

a. the applicant in question in sporadic land registration, or

b. the rights holder of a land parcel that has not been registered, or that has been registered but does not yet have a measurement certificate/situation drawing, or whose measurement certificate/situation drawing no longer corresponds to the actual situation, and the party controlling the land parcel in question, in systematic land registration, are required to indicate the boundaries of the land parcel in question and, if there is an agreement regarding the boundaries with the rights holders of adjacent land parcels, to install boundary markers.

(2) Determination of land boundaries as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Adjudication Committee in systematic land registration, and by the Head of the Land Office or a Land Office employee assigned by him in sporadic land registration.

(3) If the survey applicant or land rights holder is unable to attend at the designated time to indicate the boundaries of their land parcel as referred to in paragraph (1), the boundary designation may be delegated to another person by written authorization.

(4) If the installed boundary markers do not align with the boundary determination results as referred to in paragraph (2), the survey applicant and the relevant rights holder must move the boundary markers in accordance with the established boundaries.

(5) The boundary determination is recorded in the Minutes of Legal Data Research and Boundary Determination (list of contents 201).

(6) If boundary determination is accompanied by boundary demarcation, the results of the boundary demarcation are recorded in the Boundary Demarcation Report (list of contents 201A).

(7) The boundary delineation as referred to in paragraph (6) must be approved by the relevant rights holder and this approval must also be stated in the Boundary Delineation Minutes.

19A
(1) The applicant shall install boundary markers after obtaining the consent of the adjacent landowner.

(2) To install boundary markers as referred to in paragraph (1), photographs of the installed boundary markers shall be taken, accompanied by information about the location, coordinates, or geotagging.

(3) The installation and maintenance of boundary markers as referred to in paragraph (1) are the responsibility of the applicant.

(4) The installation of boundary markers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be outlined in a Statement of Boundary Mark Installation and Approval of the Adjacent Landowner.

(5) The photographic results of boundary markers as referred to in paragraph (2) and the Statement of Boundary Mark Installation and Approval of the Adjacent Landowner as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be required as part of the application.

(6) The Statement of Boundary Mark Installation and Approval of the Adjacent Landowner shall be prepared in accordance with the format set out in Attachment I, which is an integral part of this Ministerial Regulation.

19B
(1) Boundary determination is carried out by a surveyor based on the Statement of Boundary Mark Installation and Approval of the Adjacent Owner.

(2) Boundary determination is carried out at the location of the land parcel to be measured, provided that:

a. the surveyor reads the Statement of Boundary Mark Installation and Approval of the Adjacent Owner in the presence of the applicant or Interested Party; and

b. the applicant indicates the boundary of the land parcel being requested.

(3) If boundary determination is carried out simultaneously with boundary delineation, the results of the boundary delineation are recorded in a Boundary Delineation Report (d.i. 201A), which is approved by the relevant Rights Holder and the adjacent owner.

(1) Boundary designation and boundary determination activities may utilize information and communication technology that takes into account cross-aspects of space and time, as well as interactive digital communication, such as Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality (AR/VR), video call/audiovisual communication applications, or other technologies.

(2) The results of boundary designation and boundary determination activities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of an Electronic Document recording.

19D
(1) For aboveground and/or underground spaces, boundary determination is carried out by a measuring officer based on a designation from the developer, in accordance with the physical boundaries of the building that has been constructed and verified against a 3-dimensional model of the constructed building.

(2) The 3-dimensional model referred to in paragraph (1) can be created by converting 2-dimensional as-built drawings, mapping results using a laser scanner, or photogrammetric mapping results in a 3-dimensional model format presented in one of the following formats: DWG, DXF, IFC, OBJ, RVT, CityGML, or other 3-dimensional presentation formats.
20
(1) In the event of a dispute regarding the boundaries of adjacent land parcels, the Adjudication Committee in systematic land registration or the Head of the Land Office/appointed surveyor in sporadic land registration shall attempt to resolve the dispute amicably through deliberation between the rights holder and the rights holder of the adjacent land. If successful, the resulting boundary determination shall be set forth in a Boundary Dispute Resolution Report (list of contents 200).

(2) If, at the time of the boundary determination and measurement of the land parcel, efforts to resolve the dispute amicably through deliberation are unsuccessful, temporary boundaries shall be established based on the boundaries that are in fact the boundaries of the land parcel in question as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) of Government Regulation Number 24 of 1997, and any objections shall be notified in writing to file a lawsuit with the Court.

(3) The determination and measurement of the temporary boundaries as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be included in form 201 and recorded on the measurement drawing.

(4) If the dispute in question is submitted to court and the court issues a legally binding decision regarding the land in question, accompanied by a Minutes of Execution, or if a settlement is reached between the parties before the announcement period as referred to in Article 26 of Government Regulation Number 24 of 1997 expires, the note regarding the temporary boundaries in form 201 and the measurement drawing shall be erased by crossing it out in black ink.

(5) Regarding land plots that, according to evidence of ownership, can be registered through a recognition of rights in accordance with the provisions of Article 24 paragraph (2) of Government Regulation Number 24 of 1997 or can be granted with certain rights to individuals or legal entities, the boundaries are determined by excluding riverbanks and land designated for roads in accordance with the Detailed Spatial Planning Plan for the area concerned.

(6) In systematic land registration, state land that will be granted rights to individuals or legal entities and has been measured before the village/sub-district area is designated as the location for systematic land registration, but for which a measurement letter has not yet been issued, will have its boundaries re-determined by the Adjudication Committee.

(1) Boundary markers are installed at each corner of the land boundary and, if deemed necessary by the officer conducting the measurement, also at specific points along the boundary line of the land parcel.

(2) For boundary corners that are clearly located because they are marked by permanently installed objects such as concrete fences, walls, or wire fence posts/reinforcing stakes, boundary markers do not need to be installed.

(1) Each plot of land whose boundaries have been determined through either systematic or sporadic land registration is assigned a Land Plot Identification Number (NIB), which is listed on the Land Plot Map.

(2) The Land Plot Identification Number (NIB) as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of 14 (fourteen) digits, namely:

a. The first 2 digits are the province code;
b. The next 2 digits are the district/city code;
c. The next 9 digits are the land plot number; and
d. The last 1 digit is the code for the land plot: surface area, aboveground area, underground area, apartment unit, or rights over surface area rights, rights over aboveground area, and rights over underground area.

(3) The Land Plot Identification Number (NIB) is the reference number used in each stage of land registration activities.

(1) Each plot of land whose boundaries have been determined through either systematic or sporadic land registration is assigned a Land Plot Identification Number (NIB), which is included in the Legal Data Research and Boundary Determination Report (list 201).

(2) The NIB as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of 13 digits, of which the first 8 digits are the code for the province, district, sub-district, and village/ward where the plot is located, and the last 5 digits are the land plot number.

(3) The land plot number in systematic land registration is a sequential number per village/ward.

(4) The land plot number in sporadic land registration is a number assigned sequentially according to the order in which boundary determination was completed.

(5) If a plot of land is located in more than 1 (one) village, each portion of the plot located in a different village is assigned a separate NIB.

(6) The NIB is a reference number used in every stage of land registration activities.

(7) Land plots that have a NIB are recorded in the land register.
