16 - General
(1) The requirements for building reliability as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (3) include requirements for safety, health, comfort and convenience.

(2) The requirements for the reliability of building structures as referred to in paragraph (1) are determined based on the function of the building.

17 - Safety Requirements
(1) The building safety requirements as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) include requirements for the building's ability to support load, as well as the building's ability to prevent and overcome fire and lightning hazards.

(2) The requirements for the building's ability to support its load as referred to in paragraph (1) are the ability of the building's structure to be stable and sturdy in supporting the load.

(3) The requirements for the building's ability to prevent and overcome fire hazards as referred to in paragraph (1) are the building's ability to provide protection against fire hazards through passive and/or active protection systems.

(4) The requirements for the building's ability to prevent the danger of lightning as referred to in paragraph (1) are the building's ability to provide protection against the danger of lightning through a lightning rod system.

(1) Protection against fire hazards is carried out using a passive protection system as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (3) which includes the stability of the structure and its elements, fire-resistant construction, compartmentalization and separation, as well as protection of existing openings to restrain and limit the speed of the spread of fire and smoke.

(2) Protection against fire hazards is carried out using an active protection system as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (3) which includes the equipment's ability to detect and extinguish fires, control smoke and fire rescue facilities.

(3) Buildings, other than residential houses, must be equipped with passive and active protection systems.

(4) Provisions regarding fire safety systems as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

(1) Protection against the dangers of lightning through a lightning rod system as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (4) is the ability of a building to protect all parts of the building, including the people inside, from the dangers of lightning strikes.

(2) The lightning protection system as referred to in paragraph (1) is a lightning protection installation that must be installed on every building which, due to its location, geographical characteristics, shape and use, has a risk of being struck by lightning.

(3) Provisions regarding the lightning protection system as referred to in paragraph (2) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

(1) The ventilation system as referred to in Article 21 is the need for circulation and air exchange which must be provided in building structures through openings and/or natural ventilation and/or artificial ventilation.

(2) Residential buildings, health services, education and other public service buildings must have materials for natural ventilation.

(1) The lighting system as referred to in Article 21 is the lighting requirement that must be provided in a building through natural lighting and/or artificial lighting, including emergency lighting.

(2) Residential buildings, health services, education and other public service buildings must have openings for natural lighting.

(3) Provisions regarding the lighting system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

(1) The sanitation system as referred to in Article 21 is a sanitation requirement that must be provided inside and outside the building to meet the need for clean water, disposal of dirty water and/or waste water, dirt and rubbish and distribution of rain water.

(2) The sanitation system in buildings and their surroundings must be installed so that it is easy to operate and maintain, does not cause harm and does not disturb the environment.

26 - Comfort Requirements

(1) The requirements for building comfort as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) include comfort for movement and relationships between spaces, indoor air conditions, views, as well as vibration and noise levels.

(2) The comfort of movement space as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the dimensions of the space and the layout of the space which provides comfort in moving within the room.

(3) The comfort of the relationship between spaces as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the layout of spaces and circulation between spaces in a building to ensure the function of the building is carried out.

(4) The comfort of indoor air conditions as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the temperature and humidity in the room to ensure the function of the building.

(5) The comfort of view as referred to in paragraph (1) is a condition where a person's personal rights in carrying out activities in their building are not disturbed by other buildings in the surrounding area.

(6) The level of vibration and noise comfort as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort determined by a condition that does not result in users and the function of the building being disturbed by vibrations and/or noise arising from either within the building or its environment.

(7) Provisions regarding comfort of movement space, inter-room relationships, indoor air condition levels, views, and vibration and noise levels as referred to in paragraph (2), paragraph (3), paragraph (4), paragraph (5), and paragraph (6) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

27 - Ease of Use Requirements
(1) The requirements for convenience as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) include convenience of access to, from and within the building, as well as the completeness of infrastructure and facilities in utilizing the building.

(2) Ease of access to, from and within the building as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the availability of facilities and accessibility that are easy, safe and comfortable, including for the disabled and the elderly.

(3) The completeness of the infrastructure and facilities as referred to in paragraph (1) in buildings for public purposes includes the provision of adequate facilities for prayer rooms, changing rooms, baby rooms, toilets, parking areas, rubbish bins, as well as communication and information facilities.

(4) Provisions regarding ease of access to, from and within buildings, as well as the completeness of infrastructure and facilities as referred to in paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

disabled and elderly access


28
(1) Ease of horizontal connections between spaces in a building as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (2) is a requirement for the building to provide doors and/or corridors between spaces.

(2) Provisions regarding the number, size and technical construction of doors and corridors are adjusted to the function of the building's space.

(3) Provisions regarding the ease of horizontal connections between spaces in a building as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are further regulated by Government Regulation.





29
(1) Ease of vertical connections in buildings, including vertical transportation facilities as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (2) in the form of providing stairs, ramps and the like as well as lifts and/or escalators in buildings.

(2) Multi-storey buildings must provide stairs connecting one floor to another, taking into account the convenience, security, safety and health of users.

(3) Parking buildings must provide ramps with a certain slope and/or other vertical access facilities taking into account user convenience and safety in accordance with applicable technical standards.

(4) Buildings with more than 5 (five) floors must be equipped with vertical transportation facilities (lifts) installed according to the needs and function of the building.
