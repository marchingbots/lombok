10 - Allocation Requirements and Building Intensity

(1) The building safety requirements as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) include requirements for the building's ability to support load, as well as the building's ability to prevent and overcome fire and lightning hazards.

(2) The requirements for the building's ability to support its load as referred to in paragraph (1) are the ability of the building's structure to be stable and sturdy in supporting the load.

(3) The requirements for the building's ability to prevent and overcome fire hazards as referred to in paragraph (1) are the building's ability to provide protection against fire hazards through passive and/or active protection systems.

(4) The requirements for the building's ability to prevent the danger of lightning as referred to in paragraph (1) are the building's ability to provide protection against the danger of lightning through a lightning rod system.

(1) Protection against fire hazards is carried out using a passive protection system as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (3) which includes the stability of the structure and its elements, fire-resistant construction, compartmentalization and separation, as well as protection of existing openings to restrain and limit the speed of the spread of fire and smoke.

(2) Protection against fire hazards is carried out using an active protection system as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (3) which includes the equipment's ability to detect and extinguish fires, control smoke and fire rescue facilities.

(3) Buildings, other than residential houses, must be equipped with passive and active protection systems.

(4) Provisions regarding fire safety systems as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3) are further regulated by Government Regulation.

15 - Environmental Impact Control Requirements

(1) The requirements for building comfort as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) include comfort for movement and relationships between spaces, indoor air conditions, views, as well as vibration and noise levels.

(2) The comfort of movement space as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the dimensions of the space and the layout of the space which provides comfort in moving within the room.

(3) The comfort of the relationship between spaces as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the layout of spaces and circulation between spaces in a building to ensure the function of the building is carried out.

(4) The comfort of indoor air conditions as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort obtained from the temperature and humidity in the room to ensure the function of the building.

(5) The comfort of view as referred to in paragraph (1) is a condition where a person's personal rights in carrying out activities in their building are not disturbed by other buildings in the surrounding area.

(6) The level of vibration and noise comfort as referred to in paragraph (1) is the level of comfort determined by a condition that does not result in users and the function of the building being disturbed by vibrations and/or noise arising from either within the building or its environment.

(7) Provisions regarding comfort of movement space, inter-room relationships, indoor air condition levels, views, and vibration and noise levels as referred to in paragraph (2), paragraph (3), paragraph (4), paragraph (5), and paragraph (6) are further regulated by Government Regulation.
